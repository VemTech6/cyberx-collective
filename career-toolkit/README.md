This folder covers the part of job-hunting that isn't technical skill: getting a cyber résumé past an ATS, turning lab work into a portfolio a recruiter will actually click, and prepping for both behavioral and technical interview rounds — including the federal hiring process most Morgan State cyber students eventually run into.

<a id="-résumé"></a>
## 📝 Résumé

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Every bullet should read **tool → scope → outcome**, not a job duty. "Responsible for network monitoring" tells a recruiter nothing. "Monitored 200+ endpoints with Splunk, cut alert triage time 30% by tuning correlation rules" tells them exactly what you can do.

- **No professional experience yet?** Lead with lab work, CTF placements, and club projects — [start-here](../start-here/README.md) first projects count. List them under "Projects," not "Experience," and describe them the same way: tool, scope, outcome.
- **ATS basics:** standard section headers (Experience, Education, Skills, Projects), no tables or text boxes, .docx or plain-text-parsable PDF, keywords pulled straight from the job posting.
- Three starter templates are in [`templates/`](templates/) — copy one, don't reformat from scratch.

<a id="-portfolio"></a>
## 💻 Portfolio

Coursework and lab write-ups only count if someone can find and understand them. Turn each one into its own repo with a README that states what the project does and what you learned in the first two lines — a recruiter decides whether to keep reading in about five seconds. Screenshots and a plain-English summary beat a wall of raw config files every time.

<a id="-linkedin--github-checklist"></a>
## 🔗 LinkedIn & GitHub Checklist

- [ ] Profile photo, not a group photo or logo
- [ ] Headline states your target role, not just "student at Morgan State"
- [ ] GitHub bio + pinned repos match what you want to be hired for
- [ ] Every pinned repo has a README (see Portfolio above)
- [ ] LinkedIn "About" section mentions CyberX Collective and any certs in progress

<a id="-interview-prep"></a>
## 🎤 Interview Prep

<details>
<summary><strong>Behavioral (STAR method)</strong></summary>

Structure every answer as **Situation, Task, Action, Result**. Cyber-flavored prompts to practice out loud:

- Tell me about a time you found a problem no one asked you to look for.
- Describe a time you had to explain a technical risk to someone non-technical.
- Walk me through a project that didn't go as planned — what did you do next?
- Tell me about a time you had to learn a new tool quickly under a deadline.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Technical</strong></summary>

- **Networking:** Explain the TCP three-way handshake. What's the difference between TCP and UDP, and when would you choose each? Walk through what happens when you type a URL into a browser.
- **OS internals:** What's the difference between a process and a thread? Explain how DNS resolution and ARP work together on a local network.
- **CIA triad, applied:** A hospital's patient database goes down during a ransomware event. Walk through Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability trade-offs in your response — what do you prioritize first, and why?

</details>

<a id="-federal-hiring"></a>
## 🏛️ Federal Hiring

> [!NOTE]
> Many Morgan State cyber students end up here — federal hiring runs on different rules than private-sector applications.

- **USAJOBS vs. private applications:** federal postings list a specific grade (e.g. GS-07) and require you to address every qualification bullet directly in your résumé — vague phrasing gets auto-screened out.
- **Federal résumé format:** longer than a private-sector résumé (2–4+ pages is normal), includes supervisor contact info, hours worked per week, and exact employment dates.
- **Clearance process, high level:** background investigation → interviews with references/associates → adjudication. Some roles add a polygraph. Timeline is commonly 6–12+ months — apply early, and don't let it stop you from applying at all.
